The platform was empty except for a stray dog and a man in his fifties, suitcase by his side. Arjun sat on the cold bench, waiting for the 12:45 train. He hadn't slept in three days.

The train arrived late, as trains often do at midnight. He found an empty compartment and settled in, staring out at the darkness.

Two stations later, an elderly woman boarded. She had kind eyes and a small bag of oranges. Without asking, she sat across from him and offered one.

"You look tired, beta," she said.

Arjun almost cried. It had been years since someone had spoken to him with such tenderness.

They talked for hours — about her late husband, about his failed business, about the strange comfort of train journeys. When she got off at her stop, she pressed a ten-rupee coin in his palm.

"For luck," she said. "And remember, every darkness ends with a new morning."

The train moved on. Arjun looked at the coin, then out at the sky. For the first time in years, he smiled.
