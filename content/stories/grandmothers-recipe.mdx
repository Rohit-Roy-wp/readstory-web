The kitchen smelled of cardamom and ghee. Aarti stood at the stove, staring at the handwritten recipe card in her hand. Her grandmother's handwriting — small, slanted, filled with tiny notes in the margins.

> *"Always use warm milk, never cold."*
> *"Sing while you stir — the love goes into the food."*
> *"Don't rush. The best dishes take time."*

Aarti had never made this dish before. It was her grandmother's famous kheer — a dessert that had filled every family gathering for decades.

She followed each step carefully. Warm milk. Sing softly. Don't rush.

When it was ready, she took the first spoonful. And burst into tears.

It was exactly how her grandmother used to make it.

She realised then — recipes are not just instructions. They are love letters. Passed down, spoon by spoon, from one generation to the next.

That night, she wrote her own notes in the margin.

For her daughter, one day.
