Ramesh had been the night watchman at the old mill for twenty-three years. Every night, from 10 PM to 6 AM, he patrolled the empty corridors with a torch and a wooden stick.

Nothing ever happened. Nothing ever changed.

Except for the woman.

She appeared at exactly 2:47 AM every night — never a minute earlier or later. She wore a white saree, stood by the broken window on the third floor, and stared out at the river.

Ramesh never approached her. He simply watched from a distance, every night, for years.

One night, he finally gathered the courage. He climbed the stairs, torch shaking in his hand.

"Madam?" he called out. "Kaun ho aap?"

She turned. Her face was calm, sad, familiar.

"You don't remember me, Ramesh?" she said softly. "You were the one who pulled me out of the river. Forty years ago."

His knees buckled. He remembered — a young bride who had jumped, a young man who had failed to save her.

"I've been waiting," she said. "To thank you. For trying."

She faded. And the next morning, Ramesh resigned. He never watched the river again.
