The clock in the hallway had stopped at 3:15 — the exact time my grandfather had died. My grandmother never fixed it. "Why should I?" she'd say. "Some moments deserve to stay still."

I didn't understand then. I was eight.

Twenty years later, I returned to the house. My grandmother had passed too. The house was empty, silent, waiting to be sold.

The clock was still at 3:15.

I sat on the stairs and remembered — the smell of her cooking, her laughter, the way she hummed old songs while gardening. The way she would stop whatever she was doing when I walked in, just to hug me.

I looked at the clock again. And for the first time, I understood.

Not all broken things are broken. Some are just paused. Waiting.

I didn't sell the house. I moved in.

And every day at 3:15, I stop whatever I'm doing.

Just to remember.
