Every Sunday, my father and I would fly kites on the terrace. His favourite was a blue one — handmade, with a long red tail. "Blue is the colour of the sky," he'd say. "And the sky has no limits."

He died when I was twelve. The kite was packed away in a box, along with his old watches and reading glasses.

Twenty years later, while cleaning the storeroom, I found the box. The kite was still there, faded but intact. On an impulse, I took it to the terrace.

I hadn't flown a kite since childhood. My hands fumbled with the string. Then a gust of wind lifted it — higher and higher, until it was just a speck against the blue.

I stood there, tears streaming down my face, remembering his laughter.

"Blue is the colour of the sky," I whispered. "And the sky has no limits."

For a moment, he was right beside me again.
